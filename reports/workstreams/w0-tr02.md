**CONFIRMED: all three acquired wrappers delegate to an authored ddmin reducer.**
The label must stay. The provider chose a method name and a wrapper shape; the

## 3. What the attestations do and do not cover

`verdicts.json` carries four `live_acquisition` legs, all `pass`. Read literally,
what each one asserts:

| leg | what it actually establishes |
|---|---|
| `declared_model_matches_freeze` | The three persisted construction requests recorded the same model string the freeze pinned. A string comparison. |
| `bound_equals_candidate` | `candidate_digest == bound_digest == source_digest` for each of the three. Internal consistency of the export. |
| `executed_bytes_are_the_bound_policy` | sha256 of each executed `executed_source` equals the arm's bound policy digest, and the bound equals the candidate. Internal consistency. |
| `dispatch_was_live_not_a_recording` | The three operation rows have `outcome=success`, `model_meta_present=true`, non-null token counts, and a `gw:` receipt identity, and their persisted model matches the freeze. |

`operations.json` corroborates the fourth: the three successful construction ops
carry `input_tokens` 1522, 1452, 1772 and `model_calls: 1` each
(`cost.json` totals `input_tokens: 4746`, `output_tokens: 4551`).

**What these attest.** That this study's own export is internally consistent,
that three operations were recorded as successful gateway dispatches against the
pinned free route, and that they consumed real tokens. That is a meaningful
distinction from the recording double the transfer brief §3 describes at C15,
and the `dispatch_was_live_not_a_recording` leg is the specific gate that
separates them.

**What these do not attest.**

- That the gateway identity is genuine. `receipt_identity` is
  `gw:<operation_id>`, a locally minted string. It is not a provider-signed
  receipt, a trace ID, or anything the provider can confirm.
- That the returned bytes came from the named model. `model_meta_present: true`
  and the model string are both what the client wrote. Nothing here binds the
  response to a provider-side request ID.
- `billed` and `charge_units` are `null` on every settled receipt.
  `cost.json` says so explicitly: "every settled gateway receipt carries
  billed=null and charge_units=null, so the token counts are known and the charge
  is not. **This is not zero.**" `provider_billing.status` is `UNMEASURED`.
- The three lost responses are `UNCERTAIN` and permanently so.
- Nothing attests that these were the only three calls. `guard.ceiling` was 12
  and `ceiling_reached` is `false`, so 6 of 12 dispatches were spent. The export
  covers the run it describes; it is not a provider-side audit.

**What reproducing that history independently would require.** Reconstructing
`prompt_digest` for each operation from the frozen prompt bytes, replaying the
same dispatch to the same model and comparing the returned bytes to
`response_digest`, and reading the gateway's own request log for the operation
to confirm the response is a real provider response rather than a locally
produced one. That needs provider-side access, which this lane does not have and
must not attempt. The review correctly says it did not do this. Neither did I.

The honest statement is: **the export is self-consistent and carries the marks of
a live dispatch, and that is as far as the evidence reaches.** A reviewer who
wants the history reproduced must go to the provider and the original database.
Until then the claim is "recorded as live and internally consistent", which is
stronger than a recording double and weaker than a witnessed call.

## 5. What "312 rows" actually is

This is the part that matters, and the answer is that **312 is a refusal count,
not a result count.**

`312` traces to exactly one field: `swe.rows` in
`reports/evidence/inv_r1_aa1_e1_world_fit/result.json:1004`, and the same key in
`reports/evidence/inv_r1_w1_e1_gates/result.json:1005`. It is **not** a count
of construction events, not a count of unique source programs, and not a count of
retained artifacts. It is a count of **retained row records in the SWE
measurement panel** — and specifically, of the rows that produced nothing.

Decomposition, straight from the same artifact's `swe.representations`:

| representation | n | repaired | unrepaired | refused |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `python-step` | 156 | 124 | 32 | 0 |
| `typed-ast` | 156 | 0 | 0 | **156** |
| `action-graph` | 156 | 0 | 0 | **156** |
| **total** | **468** | 124 | 32 | **312** |

And from `reports/evidence/inv_r1_e1_swe_ceiling/matrix.json`, the row-level
census over all 468 rows:

```
outcome census: {'refused': 312, 'repaired': 124, 'unrepaired': 32}
refused by representation: {'typed-ast': 156, 'action-graph': 156}
rows with empty actions_emitted: 312
```

**312 = 156 typed-ast refusals + 156 action-graph refusals.** Both of those
representations produced **zero** repairs and **zero** unrepaired. Every
repaired row in the entire 468-row panel is a `python-step` row.

The source says this outright. `s09_swe_experiment.py:1715-1719`:

```python
def _refusal_table(rows: list) -> dict:
    """One entry per distinct refusal, keyed by its fingerprint.

    An unsupported representation carries the same refusal on every
    instance, so 312 rows hold two strings.
    """
```

Two strings, 312 rows. The 312 rows are not 312 pieces of evidence. They are
312 instances of **two** failure messages, replicated across a panel.

### The brief's sentence is false against its own artifact

`reports/STAGE-09-HANDOVER.md:99`, which the transfer brief compresses into

Three edits I would make to the brief's E1 row, in priority order:

1. **Delete "312 rows" or restate it as "312 of 468 rows refused".** As written
   it inverts the result. If it stays, it must name its denominator and its
   outcome.
2. **Fix or delete "0 refusals, 0 missing cells, 0 repairs"** at
   `STAGE-09-HANDOVER.md:99`. It contradicts the artifact at three points.
3. **Add "wrappers delegate to authored ddmin reducers"** to the E1a row
   verbatim. It is the single most load-bearing caveat on the live row, and a
   reader of the brief alone will not find it.

And keep the two framing errors I found in the brief, corrected wherever the
brief is reused:

- "The model chose `ddmin` unprompted on all three arms" — true, and worth
  keeping. Add that `reduce_software`/`reduce_graph` make `method` a required
  keyword with no default precisely so the choice is testable, and note that the
  choice is still uninformative because the alternative was one enum value.
- "the control chose `greedy` on all three" — true on the three **paired** tasks
  only. The control chose `ddmin` on both unpaired software tasks, and those are
  the two whose acquisitions were lost. The sentence reads as though the control
  were a single policy; it is a per-task authored arm.
