# TR-05: E3 and the completion record require a scoped interpretation

**Lane** `w0-tr05`, read-only, at `dfbd557`. **Verdict: PARTLY-CONFIRMED.**

The contradiction TR-05 describes is real, and it is worse than a prose slip. Two
independent generators produce it, not one. The handover compares a *shared-instance*
measurement against a *fresh-cell* measurement, and then compares both against an oracle
scored on a *sum* where the ladder is scored on a *mean*. Recomputing from source records
reproduces every disputed number exactly and locates each one.

Along the way I found a defect TR-05 does not name. The oracle yardstick
(`agenda_policy._score_constant_rules`) carries the same shared-instance bug that
retracted `crossover`, and the committed 196-rule competence table is generated from it.
The table's *conclusions* survive the defect; its *denominator labels* do not.

## 3. How the contradiction arose

The handover sentence (`reports/STAGE-09-HANDOVER.md:163-166`):

> The agenda beats `DEFAULT_RULE` at both and loses to the best rule in its own family at
> both (0.419, 0.526); at 20 it is *behind* the default, **0.157 against 0.253**.

Those four numbers come from three different identities:

| number | identity | path |
|---|---|---|
| **0.157** | agenda, budget 20 | `crossover()`, **shared instance** |
| **0.253** | historical default, budget 20 | `crossover()`, **shared instance** |
| **0.419** | best-in-space, budget 20 | `control_competence`, **fresh per rule but sum-denominated** |
| **0.526** | best-in-space, budget 30 | same, sum-denominated |

So the sentence is not self-contradictory. It is comparing a shared-walk measurement
against a differently-denominated oracle, and reading the disagreement as a finding about
the agenda. Both halves are correctly computed. They are not on a common scale.

**0.419 against 0.157 is a 2.7x gap that is entirely denominator.** Three worlds summed
against three worlds averaged is exactly a factor of three: 0.419/3 = 0.1398, and the
agenda's fresh-cell pooled mean at 20 is 0.0837, which is genuinely below 0.1398. The
"agenda loses to the best rule in its own family" claim survives correction. The margin
does not, and 0.419 was never a number the agenda could have been compared to on the
ladder's own terms.

The transfer brief (`reports/STAGE-09-TRANSFER-BRIEF.md:37-40`) restates the same mixture
as "The agenda leads the control at budgets 20 and 30" — which is the *shared* path's sign
at 20, read without noticing that the fresh path reverses it at 20 and 20 is not even a
budget where the fresh agenda leads the default.

## 5. The `crossover` resolution

TR-05 says the source "still labels crossover as a retracted shared-state path while
another path produces fresh cells." Both halves are accurate and they are not in conflict.
Resolved:

| | `crossover()` | `qualified_ladder()` | `e3_ladder.ladder(shared=False)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| file | `s09_e3_selection.py:168` | `s09_e3_selection.py:215` | `e3_ladder.py:144` |
| policy instance | **one per (budget, arm), shared across 3 worlds** | one per (arm, world, budget) | one per (arm, world, budget) |
| arms | agenda, default, fitted | agenda, default, fitted | agenda, default |
| competency block | no | yes | no |
| store paths | no | no | yes |
| status | **retracted**, N-80 | current | current |
| produces | `e3-crossover.json` | fitted-control record | `e3-postfix-ladder.json` |

**`crossover` is not a repaired path and will not become one.** The docstring at `:241-252`
says so explicitly: building the qualification on top of it "would produce a handicap
count and a yardstick score that are individually correct and jointly incomparable, because
one would be measured over independent cells and the other over a shared walk. So this
function builds a fresh policy per (arm, world, budget)... Repairing `crossover` itself is a
separate change to a retracted path and is not made here."

That is the correct call, and `crossover` should stay retracted. **Authoritative for a
ladder result: `e3_ladder.ladder(shared=False)` for the two-arm science and store paths,
`qualified_ladder` for the three-arm comparison and competency qualification.** They must
not be merged into one table, because they answer different questions and one of them is
about competence rather than about behaviour.

The defect is still live in the code. `crossover()` at HEAD reproduces the retracted
artifact byte for byte (I reran it and got identical values at all six budgets). N-413
stands: the roadmap credits `52235a6` with a fix that landed in `sever_control()` instead.
Any claim of the form "E3 at budget N" is a claim about `crossover` unless the speaker names
the fresh path.

## 6. What the recomputation changes about the result

**The narrow supported result, retained.** On fresh cells, over three qualified worlds, with
the frozen measure set: the agenda leads the historical default on held-out reduction at
budgets **14, 20 and 30**, and the default leads at **8, 40 and 60**. The agenda leads on
retained behaviours at 5 of 6 budgets. The direction reverses with the envelope, and it
reverses by 0.215 and 0.185 at the two largest budgets. This is a two-sided result with no
benefit claim, and it is the correct thing to carry into W3.

**What is withdrawn.** Any sentence containing "0.157", "0.253", "0.419" or "0.526" as an
E3 result. The first two are shared-instance; the last two are sum-denominated. None of the
four may be compared to any of the others, and none may be compared to a number from
§4b.

**What does not change.** The finding that `DEFAULT_RULE` is not the strongest rule in its
own space, and the counts that establish it. Both survive recomputation intact.

**One correction to the source's own framing.** `reports/evidence/inv_r1_e3_fitted_control/RESULT.md:164-168`
reports the fitted control as "strictly stronger than the historical one on its own
objective and strictly weaker on the reported one" and treats that divergence as "the
result." On the fresh path the fitted control beats the historical default on held-out at
budgets 20 and 30 and loses at 40 and 60, so the divergence is not directional — it is a
disagreement between two objectives with no consistent winner, which is the real and more
useful finding: **a control's competence is relative to a named objective, and on this
substrate the two candidate objectives disagree at every budget.** The fitted control is
optimal in its own space at 20 and 30 and beaten by 8 and 84 rules at 40 and 60. Neither
fixed arm is uniformly competent, and the source already says this at
`STAGE-09-HANDOVER.md:155-160`. The RESULT.md phrasing understates it.

## 7. Corrected W3/E3 comparison, ready to freeze

### Policy under test

`agenda_policy.AgendaPolicy` (`agenda_policy.py:92`), version
`ad01-policy-portfolio-agenda-v1`. The claim under test is narrow and is stated as:

> On the AD01 finite-task worlds, at a given allocation envelope, choosing each
> investigation from observed diagnostic evidence produces different held-out outcomes
> from pre-committing the same envelope to a fixed schedule, in a direction that reverses
> with the envelope.

No general autonomous-selection claim. No agency claim. The result is two-sided and
reversing, and the spec must say so before the numbers, not after.

### Arm 1 — the specified default (historical, retained)

`FixedPolicy.DEFAULT_RULE = {"software": ("ddmin", 5), "graph": ("ddmin", 1)}`.
Pre-committed, observes nothing at run time, schedule is 6 investigations wide.
**Report it, do not claim it is the strongest rule in its family.** At this tip it is not
optimal at 20, 30 or 60. It is retained because the committed evidence was produced under
it and rewriting it retroactively would make that evidence unreadable.

### Arm 2 — the competent development-fitted allocation control (the real control)

This is the control the comparison is decided against, and it is
`agenda_policy.fitted_fixed_rule(budget)`.

**How it is fitted.** Exhaustive search over all 196 constant rules — both capabilities,
both methods, depths 1-7, the same ladder the agenda walks. One rule per budget, chosen
before the run.

**On what data.** `FITTED_OBJECTIVE = "retained_behaviors"` (`agenda_policy.py:287`) —
development episodes the checker returned preserved for. **It does not read
`held_out_reduction`.** `objective_reads_held_out: False` is recorded on every fitted rule
and asserted by `test_the_fitted_rule_is_not_a_held_out_oracle`, mutation-verified red
under `FITTED_OBJECTIVE = "held_out_reduction"`.

**Inputs fixed before the run.** `budget`, `worlds`, `depths`, `methods`, `objective` —
recorded on the payload and asserted by `test_the_fitted_rule_depends_only_on_pre_run_inputs`.
The rule is a pure function of those, memoized, and returned through `_copy_fitted` so a
caller cannot mutate the cached rule in place. It observes no agenda, no candidate ranking,
no diagnostic observation, no held-out score, so **no decision the treatment made can reach
it.** `test_the_control_cannot_observe_run_time_state` mutation-verifies this red.

**Why it is a control and not an oracle.** It reads the reported metric nowhere. At budget
60 it reaches 0.187032 where an exhaustive held-out search reaches 0.414277. That gap is the
proof it is not a held-out optimum, and it should be reported as a measured fact rather
than an assurance.

**Known limit, stated up front.** It is optimal in its own space at 20 and 30 and beaten by
8 and 84 rules at 40 and 60. It is not a uniformly competent control, and neither is the
default. The spec must not present either as a strawman or as a ceiling.

### Arm 3 — held-out best-in-space, LABELED A RETROSPECTIVE REFERENCE

`constant_rule_search` / `control_competence`, objective `held_out_reduction`.
**Explicitly NOT a deployable control.** It reads the reported metric to rank rules, which
no run-time control may do. It exists to qualify the arms and to bound how much of the
agenda's or the control's headroom selection could have captured.

Required labeling on every table it appears in:
- `retrospective reference, not a deployable control`
- `reads_the_reported_metric: True`
- the world set and the denominator (mean over the three qualified worlds)
- the count of rules tied at the best score (84 at 20, 10 at 30, 8 at 40, 4 at 60 — a
  "best" that 84 rules share is not a sharp ceiling and must not be quoted as one)

**No cell comparing an arm to the oracle may be reported as a win.** The corrected numbers
put the agenda below the oracle at every budget from 20 up.

### Traceability — every decision into admitted operations and outcomes

Each arm's decisions must be traced into the store, not merely counted in memory.

- Every decision admitted → an `operation_id` under a run-scoped study root.
- Every operation → a receipt read back **by SELECT** from the receipts table, with
  `settled: true` and a decision attached.
- Every read-back receipt → matched against the policy's own `run_decisions`, not against
  the binding that produced it. A receipt compared to its own binding is a row against
  itself, and `test_s09_multi_receipt.py` exists because that was the defect.
- Counts scoped to the run's operation ids. N-301 is open: `operations_written_by_the_run`
  is `count(*)` before-minus-after over the whole table, so a foreign lane's row is
  credited to E3. **Do not quote that metric as "written by the E3 path itself" until it is
  scoped.** Use the durable-name-prefix count, as `RETRACTED.md:74-78` does, and say which
  one you used.
- The store's own contamination scanner sees only the first of the two arrival paths
  (`authority.admit_study_call` and `selection.DecisionRecorder`). Report the count under
  each and do not sum them silently.

**Show which choices the system actually made.** `picks_differ` is true at 6 of 6 budgets on
the fresh path, and the pick list (`capability_id`, `target`, `max_queries`, `charge`,
`rationale`) must be published per cell. A result where the arms made the same choices would
be vacuous; the current record shows they do not, and the evidence is the pick list, not a
summary of it.

### Fresh evaluation tasks

The portfolio must be a **fresh evaluation task set**, distinct from the development targets
the fitted control was fitted on. The current worlds use the same `dev` membership for both
the fit and the run (`portfolio_for_world` returns `membership["dev"]`), so the fitted
control is fitted and evaluated on the same targets. That is a real limitation and it
inflates the fitted control. The spec must either hold out a separate evaluation world set or
state the overlap explicitly and bound the claim to it.

### Two standing prohibitions

1. **A scripted campaign order is not an autonomous agenda.** `FixedPolicy.plan()` walks a
   pre-committed list; `AgendaPolicy` chooses from observed evidence. The distinction is the
   entire subject of the experiment. Never present the fixed arm's result as evidence about
   agenda behaviour, and never present a scripted order as a competing "policy" that
   demonstrates agency.
2. **General agency cannot be inferred from a single finite benchmark.** Three worlds, six
   budgets, one frozen measure set, one substrate. The result is a bounded claim about
   AD01 worlds at these envelopes. A green E3 does not license a statement about autonomous
   selection in general, in either direction.
