# Learner revision, consolidation and evaluation continuity

Stage 8.7 design refinement; not implemented or validated. This develops LEARN-6/8/9 and E6/E7 using the existing [learning model](LEARNING-MODEL.md), [agenda](AUTONOMOUS-AGENDA.md), [representation](REPRESENTATION-AND-TRANSFER.md) and [team](TEMPORARY-TEAMS.md) contracts. It supplies design semantics, not an additional assignment for the current engineering worker.

## 1. What self-improvement means here

Let Gamma describe the current solver repertoire, interpreters and released selection policies. Let L be a versioned procedure that uses experience and a remaining allocation to propose a change, seek information or stop. Running L produces a developmental trajectory: changes, experiments, costs, exposures, dispositions and later task outcomes.

Improving Gamma does not by itself establish that L improved. Improving L requires evidence that the changed development procedure produces better learning trajectories under a declared comparison. Merely editing the file that implements development establishes neither result.

Select **versioned policy interventions evaluated as complete bounded trajectories**. A policy can change which explanations are investigated, how constructors are prompted, how candidates are checked, how contexts are built, how teams are formed or when work stops. These can be ordinary executable packages using model calls. No model-weight update or infinite hierarchy of meta-agents is required.

| Revision scope | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Per-attempt plan and settings | Adapt within the existing envelope, recording material changes and actual execution configuration. |
| Methods, representations, context/routing/team/agenda policies | Propose, isolate, compare and release for a supported scope. |
| Development procedure and evaluator implementation | Compare trajectories or establish evaluator validity and migration obligations, respectively. |
| Ultimate authority, hard limits, protected evaluation access and recovery control | External authorization boundary; cognitive success cannot rewrite it. |

The learner can propose changes to itself because it can output a new policy artifact. The incumbent orchestrates the comparison and remains active until a valid disposition. Promotion changes future default selection; it does not let the candidate execute its own installation into the trusted controller.

## 2. The first intervention should be narrow enough to interpret

Choose a concrete observed development bottleneck after the engineering audit. Examples include repeated construction failures from a missing contract, expensive redundant experiments, poor continuation decisions or missed applicable methods. Select one principal change to the development procedure when possible. When interactions are essential, compare the whole composition and acknowledge limited component attribution.

Distinguish three hypotheses:

1. A changed solver is better on the current tasks.
2. A changed learner produces better solvers from the same starting state.
3. The changed learner helps from another starting state or in another task family.

The second and third cannot be inferred from the first. Nor can a policy claim transfer by carrying a previously learned target solution into its starting archive. Record what transfers: search procedure, task artifacts, evidence, prompts, model configuration or some explicit combination.

Related work explores editable improvement procedures and archived alternatives, but does not remove these experimental obligations. See [research notes](COGNITIVE-RESEARCH-NOTES.md), particularly [HyperAgents](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19461v1) and [Darwin Gödel Machine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954). Their results motivate testing this mechanism; they do not establish Settlement's efficacy.

## 3. Whole-trajectory comparison

Compare incumbent L0 and candidate L1 from equivalent declared Gamma0, experience/access state, model availability and total resource envelope. Use separate mutable worlds/archives. Share immutable initial material symmetrically. Each arm acquires its own subsequent observations and pays for its failures, coordination and interpretation; do not silently import a useful discovery across arms.

Freeze before evaluation exposure:

- Initial capabilities and evidence, including any material embedded in policy code or prompts.
- Development task distribution or task-generation protocol and access policy.
- Total resources, allocation between development and assessment, checkpoints and stopping rules.
- Candidate-selection rule within each trajectory and treatment of unfinished/invalid operations.
- Protected evaluation task groups, primary outcome, useful-gain threshold, permitted regressions and allowed conclusion strength.
- Adaptation allowed inside a run and source/configuration identities that define each policy.

Choose checkpoints by cumulative resource allowance rather than number of generated candidates. At a checkpoint, evaluate the currently selected composition under a frozen policy. Do not select the endpoint retrospectively using protected test performance. Intermediate assessments whose outputs reach the learner become part of its information exposure and cost; purely evaluator-side observations remain hidden and must not alter the run through an unrecorded stopping decision.

For an initial finite-panel comparison, use final supported task performance as the primary outcome and record learning speed at predeclared checkpoints, old-scope regressions, use cost and total expenditure as secondary outcomes. A speed-focused study can choose a resource-weighted performance-curve summary instead, but must freeze that choice first. Do not choose whichever summary makes the candidate look best after observing results.

Count proposals, discarded code, diagnostics, construction, context, probes, grading, retries, selection and retention/migration work within the declared acquisition envelope. Report evaluation overhead, external billing knowledge and held liability explicitly. Repeated checkpoints from one lineage and related tasks are not independent replications.

This defines a descriptive comparison. Inferential promotion still requires the executable statistical procedure and validation against the actual adaptive selection/stopping process required by LEARN-6. No default confidence interval, repeated best-of-k result or checkpoint count substitutes for that procedure.

## 4. Stop recursive evaluation at a declared experimental boundary

A candidate learner can generate descendants inside its trial allocation. Its internal self-revision rule belongs to the treatment. It may not extend the outer budget, expose the protected tasks or redefine the criterion that promotes the lineage. A trial therefore has a finite root even when its internal proposal mechanism refers to itself.

Preserve exact initial, intermediate and selected versions. Record which source lineage produced each policy and the feedback it saw. A useful failed branch may remain a bounded experimental alternative, but it does not become a released default simply because a later descendant succeeded.

When evaluating a transferred improvement policy, begin another trajectory with a declared starting solver and target-domain information. State whether the policy remains fixed to isolate its learning effect or can further revise itself. These answer different questions. Better task solving and better capacity to develop task solvers must not be merged into a single self-improvement label.

The fixed outer boundary is a deliberate mechanism for measuring a bounded intervention. It is not a theorem that the current evaluator, charter or recovery design is perfect. Their revisions have separate authority and validation requirements.

## 5. Consolidation changes the active repertoire

Growing every artifact forever into the active context/router can make more experience reduce effective competence. Select consolidation as another bounded development intervention: merge reusable structure, specialize an overgeneral method, improve an applicability description, remove an unused active route or retire a dominated version.

Maintain distinct treatments:

| Material | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Released default or specialist | Available through its scoped selector and evidence dependencies. |
| Experimental alternative | Not a default; retained with a concrete next probe, finite allocation or explicit preservation reason. |
| Historical evidence and referenced versions | Preserved according to evidence/recovery obligations; not automatically loaded into every decision. |
| Derived indexes and summaries | Rebuildable views whose source identity and freshness remain inspectable. |
| Disposable scratch | Reclaim only after checking that no active or preservation obligation depends on it. |

Retirement removes eligibility for new use; archival or byte deletion is a separate operation. An active attempt, release, warrant or reproducibility obligation can require retaining old material. If protected data cannot fit the available storage, expose that conflict and obtain resources or an authorized scope change; silently replacing it with a summary destroys the original obligation.

A consolidation proposal identifies affected versions, shared behavior, interpretation changes, active dependencies, expected use horizon, migration cost and fallback. Compare original and consolidated routes on both reuse and boundary cases. Shorter code or a smaller context is evidence of compression, not preserved behavior or net usefulness.

Avoid a global winner score. Keep scoped specialists when task-conditioned routing earns their cost. A rarely used but necessary fallback must not disappear merely because a recent task mix did not select it. Conversely, claimed future stepping-stone value has a bounded trial or revisit condition, not permanent active status.

## 6. Evaluator revision must preserve the meaning of conclusions

An evaluator is an instrument with a stated task obligation, not a privileged model opinion. It can contain bugs, weak checks or a deliberately approximate judgment. A candidate may propose a revised instrument, but cannot establish its validity using only the results it assigns to itself.

Two cases require different handling:

**Implementation correction under the same obligation.** Use independently justified positive/negative controls, discrepant cases and source-level reasoning or proof where possible. Record which historical results are affected. Re-evaluate retained source evidence when available; otherwise mark affected conclusions unsupported or qualified. Do not relabel old numbers as though the repaired evaluator had produced them.

**Changed obligation or metric.** Create a new evaluation epoch. A bridge evaluates shared identifiable cases under both meanings and states exactly which comparisons remain valid. Agreement on a finite overlap panel supports only that scope. If the new criterion measures a different objective, retain separate scores rather than invent a scalar conversion that implies the old question was answered.

Keep task generation, development feedback and protected confirmation distinct. Generated challenges may reveal errors or train a proposal policy, but shared origin can make them weak confirmation of broad improvement. When no credible independent checker exists, the system may run bounded exploratory work and improve its instruments; it must retain the narrower conclusion rather than declare scientific progress from self-agreement.

## 7. Migration and rollback semantics

Promotion binds exact policy/artifact versions, evidence epoch, scope, fallback and invalidation conditions. New work selects the promoted policy only inside that scope. In-flight work remains bound to its admitted versions unless an explicit migration is validated; quarantine follows the existing rules even for pinned attempts.

A migration must preserve pending effects, liability, commitments, evidence references and the interpretation of old artifacts. Validate or explicitly refuse migrations that change state meaning. Do not claim live upgrading of arbitrary policies merely because their function signatures match.

Rollback selects a still-valid incumbent for future use and retains the candidate's actual history. It cannot undo external effects or restore withdrawn evidence. A fallback depending on the same newly invalid premise is not a sound recovery path; re-evaluate its applicability or suspend that scope.

## 8. First rejecting probes

Before a live meta-learning comparison, test a learner that produces more candidates but no better final solver; one that hides acquisition costs; one that stops at a lucky checkpoint; one that leaks protected feedback through a summary; and one whose apparent transfer comes from embedded answers. Each should fail the relevant interpretation or admission obligation.

For consolidation, retain an old specialist required by a mixed task, reject a compressed method that erases an essential distinction, and show retirement leaves historical evidence interpretable. For evaluator migration, include a universally accepting judge, an obligation-preserving bug fix, a metric change with no valid bridge and unavailable old raw evidence. For rollback, include an incumbent invalidated by the same premise as the candidate.

These are prospective controls, not completed tests. The first useful live study should compare one observed development-policy intervention with the incumbent. Do not combine new agenda, representation, teams, learner policy and evaluator in a single initial treatment and then assign gains to each. Later whole-composition studies are legitimate when attribution is explicitly limited.

## Decisions and dependency boundary

- DLR-01: evaluate a learner by the trajectories it produces, from declared comparable starting states and total resources.
- DLR-02: permit recursive proposals inside a finite outer trial; keep promotion and protected access outside the candidate's unilateral control.
- DLR-03: treat consolidation as scoped behavioral change; separate retirement, archival and deletion.
- DLR-04: distinguish evaluator correction from changed task meaning, preserving historical attribution and explicit epoch bridges.
- DLR-05: promote and roll back future selection without pretending to undo effects, liability or evidence changes.
